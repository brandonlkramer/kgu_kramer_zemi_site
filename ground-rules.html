Ground rules

This zemi works like a research project, not a lecture class. These three rules explain what that means for you, followed by how to use Slack.

このゼミは講義ではなく、研究プロジェクトとして進めます。以下の3つのルールを守ってください。

1Work independently

You are responsible for your own research project. Before each class, check which step comes next and work on it. Class time is for feedback and discussion, not for starting the work.

自分の研究は自分で進めます。授業の前に次のステップを確認し、取り組んでおいてください。授業の時間は作業を始めるためではなく、フィードバックと話し合いのための時間です。

2Try first, then ask

Before you ask what to do, try something: write a draft, make notes, or think of two possible answers. Bring what you tried. I can give much better feedback on your attempt than on an empty page.

「何をすればいいですか」と聞く前に、まず自分で試してください（下書き、メモ、考えられる答えを2つなど）。試したものを持ってきてください。何もない状態よりも、ずっと具体的なフィードバックができます。

When you ask a question, use this pattern:

  1. What I tried: “I wrote the However sentence like this: …”
  2. Where I got stuck: “I wasn't sure whether this counts as a limitation.”
  3. My question: “Is this specific enough, or should I mention the sample size?”

3Tell me if you will be absent

Missing a class is not a problem in itself: you can check the milestone list and keep working. However, I will consider it rude if you are absent without any message. If you cannot come, send me a message on Slack before class.

欠席すること自体は問題ありません。マイルストーンを確認して、作業を続けてください。ただし、連絡なしの欠席は失礼だと考えます。出席できないときは、授業の前にSlackで連絡してください。

How to use Slack

All zemi communication happens on Slack, and almost all of it happens in the open.

ゼミの連絡はすべてSlackで行います。ほとんどのやり取りは、ほかのメンバーにも見える形で行います。

1. Use channels, not direct messages

Post everything about your research in a channel, not in a direct message (DM). Open communication is fairer and safer for everyone, and other students learn from seeing each other's questions and feedback. Use a DM only for personal matters, such as health or career plans.

2. The channels

ChannelWhat it is for
#generalGeneral messages related to research or KGU for all members of this Slack, including the Kramer zemi, Yonezaki zemi, and Izumi zemi. Use with caution.
#randomAnything else: interesting articles, events, questions for the group, casual chat. Messages here also go to all members of this Slack (Kramer zemi, Yonezaki zemi, and Izumi zemi), so use with caution.
#5_期ゼミ全体Announcements from me about this class, such as class and meeting notices. Reply here if you will be absent or late.
Your own channel
e.g. #5-Tarou		Your main research channel. Almost all exchanges about your project happen here: questions, drafts, feedback.

3. Your own channel is public

Normally only you and I write in your channel, but other zemi students (in your zemi) can read it. This is deliberate: reading a classmate's channel shows you what feedback looks like and what deadlines are coming. Feedback on drafts, slides and the thesis is given in your channel.

4. Post a summary after each meeting

After each meeting with me, post a short summary in your channel with three points:

  1. What I did since the last meeting
  2. What we discussed
  3. What I will do next

At first I will do this for you, but I expect you to take over in the 4th year.

5. Mentions and notifications

An @mention notifies that person. Set up your own notifications so that you see mentions. Do not use @channel or @everyone; everyone is expected to check Slack regularly instead.

6. Contacting me

You can mention or message me at any time, including evenings and weekends. Posting in your own channel is enough, even without a mention. I check Slack often, but I may not reply immediately.

7. React with emoji

When a message is addressed to you, react with an emoji so the sender knows you have read it.

EmojiMeaning
👀I've seen this and will look at it properly later.
🤔I've read this and will reply later.
👍 or ✅Agreed, confirmed, or done.
🙏Thank you.
💪Good luck: usually ends a conversation.

You can change the emoji above to suit the conversation and your personality. The key is to always respond!

8. Check Slack every day

Check Slack at least once a day, and assume that anything you post can be read by other zemi members.

These rules are inspired by the Slack local rules of the Watanabe Lab, Keio University: 渡辺研Slackの使い方 (kaityo256, Speaker Deck).
