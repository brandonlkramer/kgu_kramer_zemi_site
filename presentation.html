Spring presentation: “My Research Starting Point”

Spring Week 12 · about 8 minutes plus 5 minutes of questions · in English, with slides

What you will do

You will explain who you are as a researcher, what you have read, and what you might investigate. Most of the content comes from work you have already done: your introduction draft and your Worksheet 5A sheets. The new part is explaining how your reading has changed your thinking.

研究者としての自分、これまで読んだ論文、これから調べたいことを発表します。内容の多くは、序論の下書きとワークシート5Aから作れます。新しいのは「読んだことで自分の考えがどう変わったか」を説明する部分です。

Structure

PartContentBuilt fromTime
1. Who I am and what interests meYour language learning background and why this topic matters to you.Intro v11–2 min
2. Three studies I readOne Japanese paper or OASIS summary, plus your full English article. Present each in the six-box order of Worksheet 5A, with one slide per study.5A sheets4 min
3. What I learned and what is still unclearWhere the studies agree or disagree, and one question none of them answers.New1–2 min
4. My tentative questionThe research question you might investigate, in one sentence.New1 min

Questions from the audience

Every listener asks at least one question. Use these prompts if you need them:

  • Who were the participants in that study?
  • Why do you think the results were different?
  • How could you measure …?
  • Why is this topic important for learners or teachers?

How it will be assessed

Four criteria, 5 points each.

CriterionWhat a strong presentation does
Personal motivationExplains clearly why the topic matters to you.
Accuracy of summariesReports each study's method, participants and main finding correctly, in your own words.
Critical pointNames a specific limitation or open question, not a general one.
Research questionGives a question that follows from the studies and that an undergraduate could investigate.
