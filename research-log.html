Research log

One Google Sheets file with three tabs: everything you read, how each study was done, and who took part

Set up Tab 1: Reading log Tab 2: Methods Tab 3: Participants How you will use it

Set up

Create one Google Sheets file in your folder in the class Google Drive. Name it Research log – [your name] and make three tabs: Reading log, Methods and Participants.

クラスのGoogleドライブの自分のフォルダに、Googleスプレッドシートを1つ作成します。ファイル名は「Research log – 名前」とし、Reading log、Methods、Participantsの3つのタブを作ってください。

Every paper gets a number in the Reading log tab. Use the same number on its Worksheet 5A sheet and in the other two tabs, so you can always find everything about one paper.

You start the log in Fall Week 1 by entering all the papers you read in Spring. After that, add each new paper in the same week you read it.

1Reading log

One row for every paper you read.

ColumnWhat to enter
No.1, 2, 3 … (the same number as the 5A sheet)
ReferenceFull APA reference
TypeAbstract only / Japanese full paper / OASIS summary / English full article
Topic tags2–3 keywords, e.g. vocabulary, apps, high school
Main findingOne sentence
RelevanceHigh / medium / low, plus a few words on why

2Methods

One row for every paper that is relevant to your own study.

ColumnWhat to enter
No.Same as the Reading log
DesignSurvey / interview / test / experiment / observation / text analysis / mixed
InstrumentsName, number of items, scale type and source, e.g. a 20-item questionnaire on a 5-point Likert scale, adapted from …
Data collectionHow, where, when, and how long
AnalysisWhat they did with the data, in simple words, e.g. compared group averages, coded interview answers into themes
Could I use this?Yes / maybe / no, and why
Finding the instrument: look for an appendix, the source the authors cite for the questionnaire or test, or the supplementary materials on the journal website.

3Participants

One row for every paper that is relevant to your own study.

ColumnWhat to enter
No.Same as the Reading log
WhoLearner type, e.g. first-year English majors
NumberTotal, and per group if there were groups
Age, year or levelWhatever the paper reports
ContextCountry, type of institution, type of class
RecruitmentHow participants were chosen, e.g. the author's own classes, volunteers

How you will use it

  • Literature review (Fall Weeks 4–5): filter the Reading log by topic tag to see which studies belong together.
  • Choosing a method (Fall Week 8): sort the Methods tab by design to see how other researchers studied questions like yours, and which instruments appear more than once.
  • Planning participants (Fall Week 9): the Participants tab shows how many participants similar studies had and how they were recruited.
